# Pricing review, November 18, 2026

This change incorporates the defensible part of the supplied Claude bundle into the **current** Xylem model. The original model multiplied each system's connections by one 2024 State Water Board Table 7 average for its size band. That made a Failing system with 100 connections cost $13.995 million before statewide scaling and a 101-connection system $3.212 million. The At-Risk jump was $25.251 million to $2.501 million. A one-connection change should not make an otherwise identical project several times cheaper.

The revised model interpolates the published cost-per-connection averages across the observed median size in each band. It constrains cost per connection to fall, and total project cost to stay level or rise, as system size grows. It then scales the resulting **2024** estimates to the State Board's published $5.386 billion statewide 2024 total, as the existing model did. At 100 and 101 connections the revised estimates are $4.713 million and $4.723 million for Failing, and about $5.120 million for both At-Risk. This smooth curve is a modeling assumption, not a State Board price schedule. The new county median absolute percentage discrepancy is 31.8% for counties with at least $10 million in 2024 estimated need. The saved 2026 registry and forecast are unchanged.

The uploaded `xylem-system-prices.csv` and v3 bundle were examined as **proposals**, not instructions. The per-system prices in that CSV use a 1.74× grant calibration and a 2024-to-2026 escalation. They were not imported. Reasons:

- The [State Board's 2024 physical consolidation method](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/drinking_water/certlic/drinkingwater/documents/needs/2024/2024costassessment-physical-consolidation.pdf) uses 3.1% for a forecast from April 2023 to April 2024, where appropriate. It does not establish a 3.1% annual water-construction index through 2026. The [2026 Needs Assessment page](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/drinking_water/certlic/drinkingwater/needs.html) says the cost assessment was **not updated** in 2025 or 2026.
- The nine-row calibration set mixes single-system treatment, regional consolidation and well plus pipe projects. The [Springfield announcement](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2025/pr20250509-springfield.html) says its $15 million covers the existing Springfield system, a mobile-home park and 20 domestic-well households. Assigning the full sum to one listed system would inflate the inferred system price. The [Frazier Park agreement amendment](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/board_info/agendas/2025/oct/100725_10_edrpt.pdf) also identifies multiple beneficiary systems. A sample of nine funded projects selected for public reporting does not support a statewide 1.74× multiplier or a calibrated 90% interval.
- The [Riverdale 2026 announcement](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2026/pr20260528-riverdale-park-tract-water-boards.html) says construction grants **exceed** $7 million, with technical assistance separately over $1 million. The [Allensworth 2026 announcement](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2026/pr20260120-allensworth-community-new-water-system.html) reports a $3.8 million construction grant for a well and tank. The [Robbins 2025 announcement](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2025/pr20250801-SAFER-progress.html) reports an $8 million treatment facility; the announced population exceeds the saved registry population. These are credible project references, but an award or budget is neither an audited final construction price nor the remaining amount needed today.
- The bundle's grant cap table is substantively consistent with [Table 13 of the FY 2025–26 expenditure plan](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/grants_loans/docs/2025/fy2025-26-fep-final-1125.pdf), but that table caps grant/principal forgiveness by eligible residential connections and has exceptions. It is not a project construction-cost cap. We did not apply it to modeled costs.
- The supplied `cost_growth.csv` has five percentages without project identities or source links. It cannot be audited or used to estimate escalation.

The existing cost assumption slider and 0.5×–2× illustrative range remain in place. The Method tab now says the range is **not** a confidence interval and notes location, bid timing, labor, materials, treatment scope and permitting. The prior assistant's project-specific cost floors and pricing UI were removed by starting this change from current `main`.

The $500 million default plan now selects 76 systems serving 366,486 modeled people, versus 97 systems and 476,663 people in the original saved plan. These are allocation simulations, not measured intervention outcomes. Prior plan comparison files retain their original snapshots. The original files are copied into `audit/pricing-baseline/data/` so historical optimizer parity remains reproducible.

A full `pipeline/build.py` run uses the same curve when the archived training inputs are available. Run `python tools/presentation_build.py --write` after UI changes and `python tools/verify_static.py` to verify hash-bound assets. No site deployment is included.
